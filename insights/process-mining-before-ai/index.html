Process Mining Before AI: Every Time

You can't automate what you don't understand. Process mining reveals the truth about your operations — not the version in the SOP document, but the version that actually runs every day. Here's why it should always come first.

The gap between documentation and reality

Every enterprise has process documentation. Standard operating procedures. Workflow diagrams. Compliance manuals. They describe how processes should work.

They're wrong. Not intentionally. Not maliciously. They're just incomplete. Process documentation captures the "happy path" — the idealised version of how work flows when everything goes according to plan. But in real operations, things rarely go according to plan.

Process mining changes this. Instead of asking people how a process works, you extract event logs from the systems where work actually happens — your ERP, CRM, ticketing system, or core banking platform. You analyse millions of real transactions. And you discover how work actually flows.

The results are always surprising.

What process mining reveals

Across hundreds of process mining engagements, patterns emerge. Here's what we consistently find:

3-5x more process variants than expected

Stakeholders describe a process as having 3-4 steps. Process mining reveals 15-20 distinct variants. Some variants handle 40% of the volume. Others handle rare edge cases that consume 60% of the processing time.

This matters for AI because you can't build one model for one process. You need to understand the variants, identify which ones are worth automating, and design solutions that handle the real complexity of the process.

Hidden bottlenecks that don't match intuition

When you ask a team where the bottlenecks are, they point to the step that feels slowest. Process mining often reveals that the actual bottleneck is somewhere else entirely — a handoff between teams, a waiting step where work sits in a queue, or a rework loop where 25% of transactions go through the same step three times.

One financial services client was convinced their bottleneck was document review. Process mining showed that document review took an average of 12 minutes. But the handoff from document review to approval sat in a queue for an average of 3.2 days. The bottleneck wasn't the work — it was the wait.

Compliance violations hiding in plain sight

In regulated industries, process mining reveals compliance deviations that are invisible to traditional auditing. Steps executed out of sequence. Approvals bypassed. Segregation of duties violations. These aren't always intentional — they're often workarounds that teams have developed because the documented process doesn't work in practice.

Knowing about these deviations before regulators find them is enormously valuable. It's also critical for AI design — you need to know which steps are mandatory and which are workarounds.

The real cost of manual work

Process mining quantifies what manual work actually costs. Not estimates. Not averages. Actual data: how many transactions, how much time per transaction, how many errors, what each error costs to fix.

This data is the foundation of an AI business case. When you can tell a CFO "this process handles 47,000 transactions per month, each taking an average of 23 minutes, with a 7% error rate that costs $340 per error to resolve" — that's a business case. That's not a guess.

Why process mining must come before AI

Here's the core argument: if you build AI without process mining, you're building for a process that doesn't exist.

Consider what happens when you skip process mining:

  • You automate the wrong step (the one stakeholders think is the bottleneck, not the one that actually is)
  • Your model fails on variants it's never seen (because stakeholders didn't mention the 17 process variants that handle 40% of volume)
  • Your business case is based on estimates, not data (so the CFO kills the project at the first budget review)
  • Your compliance team blocks deployment (because the AI wasn't designed for the actual compliance requirements)

Process mining eliminates all of these failure modes. It gives you:

  • The right target: Data-driven identification of the highest-value automation opportunity
  • The real process: Every variant, exception, and edge case your AI needs to handle
  • The hard numbers: Quantified business case with real transaction data
  • The compliance map: Every regulatory requirement and current deviation

The process mining toolkit

Process mining is a mature discipline with enterprise-grade tools. The three major platforms are:

Celonis: The market leader, particularly strong in SAP environments. Celonis provides real-time process monitoring and can trigger automated actions based on process deviations. Best for large enterprises with complex, high-volume processes.

Signavio (SAP): Now part of SAP, Signavio combines process mining with process modelling and simulation. Strong integration with SAP environments. Good for organisations already in the SAP ecosystem.

Apromore: An Australian-developed, open-source process mining platform. Strong academic foundations. Good for organisations that want to start with lower cost or need on-premises deployment for data sovereignty.

At Stack10, we're platform-agnostic. Our process mining engagements (delivered through our partner Keystone Intelligence) work with any of these platforms. We recommend the right tool based on your environment, volume, and requirements.

A practical example

A major Australian lender asked us to "build an AI system to speed up loan processing." Their assumption: the underwriting step was too slow and needed AI-assisted decision-making.

Process mining told a different story. Underwriting averaged 14 minutes per application — fast by industry standards. The real bottleneck was document collection. Applicants were submitting incomplete documentation, triggering an average of 2.3 follow-up requests per application. Each follow-up added 4.1 days to the process.

The highest-value AI opportunity wasn't in underwriting. It was in intelligent document validation at the point of submission — catching incomplete or incorrect documents before they entered the pipeline. The AI we built checks documents in real-time, identifies missing information, and guides applicants to complete their submission correctly the first time.

Result: 73% reduction in processing time. Not because we automated underwriting, but because process mining showed us the real problem.

Start with the truth

If you're planning an AI initiative, start with process mining. Not because we sell it (we do, through Keystone Intelligence). But because it works. Every time we've seen an AI project fail, the root cause traces back to assumptions about the process that turned out to be wrong.

Process mining gives you the truth. Build your AI on truth, and it ships. Build it on assumptions, and it dies in pilot purgatory.

Every time.
