I # Homepage Flow Audit & Recommendations

## Current Section Order

```
1. Hero
2. Problem
3. Solutions (3 products)
4. Case Studies
5. Differentiators ("Five reasons...")
6. Industries
7. Commitments ("Data misuse. Vendor lock-in...")
8. CTA
```

---

## What's Broken

### 1. The hero headline is generic

`"Your Trusted AI Transformation Partner"` — every consulting firm on earth says this. "Trusted" and "Partner" are the two most overused words in B2B services. The subtitle (`"We help enterprises set and execute their AI strategy across their products, processes, and people"`) is boilerplate. There's no villain, no urgency, no differentiation in the first 10 words a visitor reads.

### 2. No bridge between Problem → Solutions

The Problem section ends on a strong note (automating dysfunction at scale). Then Solutions immediately opens with `"Three products. A clear progression."` — a clinical product catalogue. The reader went from feeling understood to reading a brochure. There's no resolution moment: *"Here's what we built to fix that."*

### 3. Case Studies are in the wrong position

They sit between Solutions and Differentiators. The logical order should be: *establish why you're credible → then prove it with evidence.* Right now you're showing proof before you've made the argument. Case studies land harder after you've told the buyer why Stack10 is different.

### 4. Differentiators and Commitments are the same section — split across the page

Both sections answer `"Why should I trust Stack10?"` — but they're separated by the Industries section. This is the most damaging flow problem. The trust argument builds momentum in Differentiators, then gets completely broken by Industries, then tries to restart with Commitments. Readers mentally move on.

### 5. The 90-day guarantee is buried in 5th place

`"Every engagement proves value in 90 days or less"` is the most concrete, differentiated claim on the page. It's sitting at the bottom of a list of five bullet points. It should be far more prominent — possibly in the hero itself.

### 6. No trust signal near the top

Nothing in the first two sections signals authority — no client logos, no `"50+ enterprises"`, no geography credibility (`"Australian-built"`), nothing. A logos bar or even a one-line social proof strip immediately after the hero would anchor everything below it.

### 7. Industries are orphaned

Sandwiched between Differentiators and Commitments with no narrative connection to either. They read like a navigation element that wandered into the page.

### 8. No "How It Works" section

The three products (Discover → Build → Scale) are presented as cards with descriptions, but there's no walkthrough of the process journey. A buyer reading this for the first time doesn't understand how the progression works in practice. This is a major source of friction.

---

## Recommended Section Order

```
1. Hero              — rewritten headline, outcome-led, villain in the subhead
2. Logos/Proof Bar   — ADD THIS: "Trusted by enterprise teams in healthcare, financial
                       services, government..." or client logos
3. Problem           — keep current content, strong
4. Bridge → Solutions — 1-2 sentences connecting problem to your response, then products
5. How It Works      — ADD THIS: simple 3-step (Discover → Build → Scale journey)
6. Case Studies      — now lands as proof after the argument is made
7. Why Stack10       — MERGE Differentiators + Commitments into one section
8. Industries        — move here as a specificity signal before the final CTA
9. CTA               — keep current content
```

---

## Specific Rewrites

### Hero Headline

Replace `"Your Trusted AI Transformation Partner"` with one of:

- `"AI that ships in weeks. Not quarters."` — outcome-led, speed differentiator
- `"We build AI. We don't advise it."` — verb-led, anti-consulting positioning
- `"Stop piloting. Start shipping."` — confrontational, memorable (echoes the CTA)
- `"Enterprise AI, designed to deliver — not to advise indefinitely."` — longer but sharper villain

### Bridge Copy (insert before Solutions grid)

> Stack10 was built in direct response to this. A firm structured to ship AI that works — not advise on it indefinitely.

### Differentiators — reorder list

Move the 90-day claim from #5 to #1. It's the hardest, most concrete differentiator on the page:

```
1. Every engagement proves value in 90 days or less   ← was #5
2. Output pricing, not hours
3. Senior engineers. No exceptions.
4. Process truth before AI
5. Validated ROI. Not promised.
```

### Merge Differentiators + Commitments

The Commitments content (`"Data misuse. Vendor lock-in..."`) is some of the best copy on the page. Rather than a separate section, fold the four Commitment cards into a second row within the Why Stack10 section:

- Row 1 label: `"How we work"`  → the 5 differentiator items
- Row 2 label: `"Built-in protections"` → the 4 commitment cards
